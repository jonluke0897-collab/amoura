# TikTok — What I Got Wrong This Week (Weeks -7 to -1, recurring)

**Use when:** A user, advisor, or community member pointed out something that needed to change — and you changed it. This is the highest-trust-building format.
**Length:** 30–45 seconds, face-to-camera, no cuts.
**Cadence:** Whenever something genuinely went wrong (~every 2–3 weeks). Faking these is worse than not posting them.

---

## Script

> **Hook:**
> "I got something wrong this week."

> **Beat 2 — What you wrote/built:**
> "Here's the [COPY / FEATURE / DECISION] I shipped: [QUOTE OR DESCRIBE IN ONE SENTENCE — e.g., 'I had a field on the profile editor that just said *Gender* with a dropdown']."

> **Beat 3 — Who pushed back and why:**
> "[ADVISOR FIRST NAME / USER] told me [SPECIFIC FEEDBACK — e.g., 'a single dropdown forces people to flatten an identity that doesn't flatten']. They were right."

> **Beat 4 — What you changed:**
> "So I changed it to [SPECIFIC NEW APPROACH]. It now [SPECIFIC RESULT]."

> **Beat 5 — Close:**
> "I'll keep getting things wrong. I'll keep telling you when I do."

---

## Caption

```
Week [N] mistake: [ONE-LINE SUMMARY].

Fixed because [ADVISOR FIRST NAME OR USER] pushed back. That's the whole point of the advisor model.

#trans #buildinpublic #datingapp
```

## Notes

- Do NOT manufacture mistakes for content. Only post when one actually happened.
- Do NOT throw the advisor or user under the bus by framing them as "harsh." Frame as: they were right, you listened, you changed it.
- If the mistake is sensitive (e.g., touched real harm), have the advisor review the script before filming.
- Resist the urge to over-apologize. State, change, move.
